Normalization audit: what can be corrected, what remains unproved
================================================================

Status and protocol
-------------------
This is a mathematical comparability audit, not a new HPO efficacy result.
Two separately named candidates were derived from symmetry requirements and
local Hessian identities. Pilot correlations were not used to select either
formula, aggregation, or transformation values. Original scores, corrections,
known failures, and all negative evidence must remain separate.

Frozen before checkpoint scoring:
  normalization_protocol.json, version 1.1
  SHA256 0f9364f9d435483dfc3cbe282483b65ceea1686094b6428da85a255acb57b44d
The unevaluated v1 draft was retained when the panel was reduced to the
parent-requested bounded set; formulas were unchanged. Another agent owns
all-16-checkpoint empirical inference. This note and normalization_identities.py
contain analytical work only: no training, test data, or pilot outcome fitting.

Set-up
------
Let h_i in R^D be a frozen penultimate activation, W in R^(D x K) the Keras
output kernel, b in R^K its bias, z_i = h_i W+b, and p_i=softmax(z_i).
The final hidden activation is ReLU in the audited models.
Let C_i=diag(p_i)-p_i p_i^T and P=I_K-11^T/K.
Write W_c=W P and z_c,i=z_i P. The original final-kernel mean-CE Hessian is
  H = (1/B) sum_i C_i tensor (h_i h_i^T)
up to a vectorization permutation. Thus
  trace(H) = mean_i (1-||p_i||^2)||h_i||^2.
No labels occur in these Hessian identities, although loss and accuracy do
depend on labels. Bias, earlier-layer weights, and regularizers are outside
this particular H.

1. Class-centering fixes an additive output gauge, not the theorem
----------------------------------------------------------------
For any v in R^D, W' = W + v 1^T adds h_i v to every class logit of example i.
All predictions and the original head Hessian are unchanged. Since 1^T P=0,
W'_c=W_c. Class-centering is the unique minimum-Frobenius-norm representative
within each such output-kernel gauge class: each row's common mean is removed.
The same centering can be applied to the bias for output-based quantities.

Replacing ||W|| with ||W_c|| in the original activation/weight ratio therefore
removes that specific arbitrary denominator effect. It does NOT remove
adjacent-layer scaling. For s>0, h'=h/s and W'=sW preserve logits, while the
centered ratio still changes by 1/s^2. Positive per-neuron diagonal scaling
creates the corresponding nonuniform dependence.

More decisively, the earlier false-lower-bound witness W=(0.1,-0.1), h=1 is
ALREADY class-centered. Centering leaves it unchanged. Its claimed bound is
5.024773 while its exact head Hessian Frobenius norm is 0.495033. Therefore
gauge centering cannot repair the published theorem even in this tiny case.

A canonical hidden-RMS normalization can remove positive per-neuron scaling:
if a_j=sqrt(mean_i h_ij^2)>0, replace h_ij by h_ij/a_j and W_c[j,:] by
a_j W_c[j,:]. This is invariant under h_j/s_j, s_j W_j for positive s_j.
But zero/dead features require a convention, the representation depends on
the reference sample, and uniform neuron duplication still changes the
activation/weight ratio by the duplication factor. This intermediate idea
was analyzed but is NOT a third empirically selectable candidate.

2. Candidate C1: gauge-centered relative-head trace
-------------------------------------------------
Define fixed row scales r_j=||W_c[j,:]||_2, D_r=diag(r_j), and

  T_rel = mean_i [(1-||p_i||_2^2) sum_j h_ij^2 r_j^2].

This has a precise local meaning. Define perturbation coordinates theta by
delta W[j,:] = r_j delta theta[j,:], holding r fixed at the current model.
The pullback Hessian is
  H_rel = (I_K tensor D_r) H (I_K tensor D_r),
and trace(H_rel)=T_rel exactly. If a row scale is zero this is a singular
coordinate map; no invertibility or full-metric claim is needed for the
identity. Differentiating r as a function of theta would be a different
quantity and is not proposed.

The weighted hidden-feature kernel
  K_rel(i,l) = sum_j h_ij h_lj r_j^2
provides another useful way to see the invariance. Under positive diagonal
rescaling h'_ij=h_ij/s_j, W'_j=s_j W_j, class-centering gives
r'_j=s_j r_j, so every product h'_ij r'_j equals h_ij r_j. Therefore the
entire K_rel and H_rel are unchanged. Common-output row shifts are removed
by W_c; hidden permutations only relabel coordinates. T_rel is invariant to
all these transformations.

For completeness, the squared Frobenius norm of this same local Hessian is
  ||H_rel||_F^2 = B^(-2) sum_i,l trace(C_i C_l) K_rel(i,l)^2.
This identity explains the kernel construction; it is not an additional
candidate selected for empirical scoring. Standard PSD inequalities apply
to H_rel, for example T_rel/sqrt(r_max) <= ||H_rel||_F <= T_rel with
r_max=(K-1)min(B,D). They do not bound the original ratio by original H.

KNOWN FAILURE — neuron duplication:
Duplicate every hidden neuron r times and divide each outgoing weight row
by r. Predictions are unchanged. Every copy has row scale r_j/r, so
K_rel' = K_rel/r and T_rel'=T_rel/r. Thus C1 still rewards an arbitrary
redundant representation. The original RMS ratio instead grows by r, and
the unweighted exact head trace/Frobenius norm also grow by r.
An unweighted hidden-width division is not a principled universal repair:
for unequal duplication or split weights alpha_q W_j with sum alpha_q=1,
this C1 contribution changes by sum alpha_q^2, not a universal width factor.
Adding cancelling hidden paths can likewise change C1 while preserving
outputs, as can redistributing a constant feature's effect into output bias.

The invariance correction is consequently partial and must be described that
way. Raw H can still be made arbitrarily large or small by a global positive
rescaling while T_rel stays fixed. No universal positive lower/upper bound
on raw H follows from T_rel without additional scale restrictions.

3. Candidate C2: temperature curvature in prediction space
---------------------------------------------------------
Define

  T_temp = mean_i [sum_k p_ik z_c,ik^2 - (sum_k p_ik z_c,ik)^2]
         = mean_i z_c,i^T C_i z_c,i
         = mean_i Var_(k~p_i)(log p_ik).

For any fixed label y_i, consider the artificial one-dimensional path
  L(alpha)=mean_i CE(softmax(alpha*z_c,i),y_i).
Then L''(1)=T_temp. The label-dependent term is linear in alpha, so the second
derivative is label-independent. This is an exact identity, distinct from
the original network's final-weight or full-network Hessian.

Centered logits are determined by probabilities because
log p_i=z_i-logsumexp(z_i)1. Thus T_temp is invariant to every exact
representation change preserving predictions: common-logit shifts, positive
adjacent-layer diagonal scaling, hidden permutation, uniform duplication,
and even cancelling-path changes. It includes the centered output bias.
It has no hidden-width dependence from redundant representations. Class
count and probability distribution can still change its scale; no
across-dataset comparability claim is proposed.

KNOWN FAILURE — invariance does not imply selection quality:
Uniform predictions have z_c=0 and T_temp=0, while binary predictions becoming
fully saturated also yield T_temp tending to zero (gap^2*exp(-gap) behavior).
Minimizing it need not favor learning. It cannot distinguish confident
correct from confident wrong predictions.

For fixed label y=0, compare p=(.99,.01) and p=(.01,.99). Both have
T_temp=.0099*(log 99)^2 = 0.2090397517273, but CE is respectively
0.0100503358535 and 4.6051701859881, and accuracies are 1 and 0.
A one-hidden-unit realization gives equal C1 and equal unweighted head
trace as well. Swapping output class probabilities here is deliberately NOT
a symmetry of the supervised task with fixed labels: it is a counterexample
to any score-to-loss/accuracy ordering implication.

Consequently this candidate is a separately named output-confidence
curvature control, not a corrected activation/weight bound. It may be
empirically evaluated in a later locked ranking study, but that usefulness
is an open question. The proposed invariance-only panel cannot answer it.

4. What the bounded empirical panel can establish
------------------------------------------------
All sixteen existing final checkpoints should be scored on exactly the same
1500 official-training inputs. No labels, validation losses, or test arrays
are needed. Fourteen prespecified cases per checkpoint include baseline,
common-row shifts, positive global and diagonal scaling, reversal of hidden
neurons, and duplication factors 2 and 4. Check centered logits and all
probabilities remain fixed within frozen numerical tolerances.

Record original scores, both distinct candidates, and unweighted head trace
for every transformation. Report C1's expected 1/r duplication change as a
substantive limitation, not an implementation failure. Preserve numerical
failures and all zero/near-zero components. Comparing transformed models
tests symmetry behavior only; it does not show early selection improvement,
causal generalization, or superiority over validation loss.

5. Reproducible deterministic evidence
-------------------------------------
normalization_identities.py uses a seeded synthetic fixture and materializes
its tiny head Hessian. The C1 trace identity agrees with this explicit
pullback Hessian. C2 agrees with a central second difference of the artificial
temperature path. Every frozen transformation, the fixed-label ordering
counterexample, and the already-centered original-theorem counterexample
are preserved in normalization_identity_results.json. All assertions passed.

The source review in primary_sources.txt separately explains why published
scale-invariant flatness/PAC-Bayes analyses do not imply this ratio bound or
cross-configuration selection guarantees. No external theorem is imported
to replace the explicit, restricted identities derived above.
