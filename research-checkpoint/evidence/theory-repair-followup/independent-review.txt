Independent review of the softmax-head repair

The finite perturbation statement is valid under the assumptions below. It gives a principled, inexpensive replacement quantity for local head sensitivity. It does not establish the original inverse-weight norm ratio, a generalization theorem, or a ranking of eventual classification accuracy. This review is an independent derivation against the exact local manuscript and prior saved counterexamples. No manuscript edit or model training was performed.

Let h_i in R^d be fixed features, W in R^(d x K), fixed biases b, z_i=W^T h_i+b, p_i=softmax(z_i), and L the unregularized sample-mean cross-entropy with normalized target distributions. Then g=mean h_i(p_i-y_i)^T, H=mean C_i tensor h_i h_i^T for column-vectorized W, C_i=diag(p_i)-p_i p_i^T, and T=tr(H)=mean (1-||p_i||_2^2)||h_i||_2^2. The features and their normalization, model evaluation mode, and sample weighting must remain fixed along the perturbation path. No stationarity, separability, or small-loss assumption is needed. These are head-block statements, not statements about the full network Hessian.

The decisive local manuscript error occurs in paper/main.tex:505-532: the Hessian derivation introduces an inverse dependence of the preceding hidden activation on the output activation while taking a partial derivative with respect to independent final weights. In this partial derivative, h_i is fixed. The preceding displayed gradient also omits y_i. Omitting y_i alone would not change this head Hessian because the label term is linear in W; the invented dependence does. The singular softmax Jacobian by itself is insufficient criticism because its rectangular product with W may have full column rank. The prior review at theory-assumptions-review/review.md and its results.json independently give a centered, stationary global-minimum witness with ||H||_F=0.48 and the claimed ratio=2.5151458962. No common-shift construction is required for that violation.

For q=softmax(z+v), the componentwise ratio q_k/p_k lies in [exp(-osc(v)),exp(osc(v))], where osc(v)=max(v)-min(v). For every vector u, u^T C(p)u=min_c sum_k p_k(u_k-c)^2. Applying the probability-ratio bound before this minimization proves exp(-osc(v))C(p) <= C(q) <= exp(osc(v))C(p) in Loewner order, including their common null direction. Since osc(Delta W^T h_i) <= sqrt(2)||Delta W||_F||h_i||_2, setting a=sqrt(2)rho R, R=max_i ||h_i||_2, and ||Delta W||_F<=rho gives exp(-ta)H(W) <= H(W+t Delta W) <= exp(ta)H(W) for 0<=t<=1. Thus the proposed exp(sqrt(2)rho R) upper factor is correct.

Integrating the exact Taylor remainder is sharper. With phi_+(a)=(exp(a)-1-a)/a^2 and phi_-(a)=(exp(-a)-1+a)/a^2, both continuously equal to 1/2 at a=0, the Bregman remainder B=L(W+Delta W)-L(W)-<g,Delta W> satisfies phi_-(a) vec(Delta W)^T H vec(Delta W) <= B <= phi_+(a) vec(Delta W)^T H vec(Delta W). In particular B equals mean_i KL(p_i(W)||p_i(W+Delta W)), exactly. The advertised upper bound using (1/2)exp(a) is valid but looser. The deterministic loss bound is L(W+Delta W)-L(W) <= rho||g||_F+rho^2 phi_+(a)T. Dropping the gradient term for an arbitrary one-epoch model would be incorrect.

For a bounded random perturbation conditional on the fitted W and fixed features, E[Delta W]=0 removes the linear term. If its vector covariance is Sigma, then phi_-(a)tr(H Sigma) <= E[L(W+Delta W)-L(W)] <= phi_+(a)tr(H Sigma). Isotropic covariance sigma^2 I gives sigma^2 T; isotropy only in class-contrast directions also gives sigma^2 T because H annihilates common-class directions. A Gaussian has unbounded support and does not meet the stated almost-sure radius condition. For a uniform sphere of radius rho in D=d(K-1) contrast coordinates, sigma^2=rho^2/D. A fixed per-coordinate variance and a fixed total perturbation radius are different conventions when widths or class counts vary; they must not be silently interchanged.

The rank sandwich is valid: if r=rank(H)>0, T/sqrt(r) <= ||H||_F <= T and T/r <= ||H||_2 <= T. For finite logits and the feature-design matrix X whose rows are h_i^T, r=(K-1)rank(X); the equality follows because every C_i is positive definite on class contrasts. An upper bound on r can replace r only in the lower bounds. If r=0, H=T=0 and division by r is inapplicable. Minimizing a lower bound alone never establishes minimization of an unknown larger quantity. T is stronger here because it is an exact trace and an upper bound on both listed norms.

If bias is perturbed, augment h_i by 1 and W by the bias row, use the joint kernel-and-bias radius, and recompute R, H, and T. A kernel-only formula does not bound arbitrary bias changes. Adding explicit L2 regularization changes the Hessian and remainder and must be accounted for separately. AdamW's training rule does not turn this unregularized-loss identity into an identity for the optimizer dynamics.

T is invariant to W -> W+v 1^T and b -> b+c 1 because those transformations preserve p_i on every input; it remains sensitive to feature parameterization. For h_i -> c h_i and W -> W/c, predictions are fixed while T -> c^2 T. A specified relative perturbation geometry, for example using class-centered row norms, can address positive feature rescaling, but its exact covariance and invariances need their own statement. Feature duplication and changing width are not automatically resolved. These restrictions matter when comparing hyperparameter candidates with different architectures.

This repair retains the screening idea at the level of cheap forward-pass probabilities and hidden-activation norms. It changes the score: 1-||p||^2 measures predictive uncertainty, while the original ratio uses ||p|| and divides by ||W||. There is no algebraic equivalence. A confident wrong prediction can have arbitrarily small T, so this label-independent quantity alone cannot guarantee low classification error. The result controls loss and prediction sensitivity under specified head perturbations at the current checkpoint; it says nothing by itself about future training trajectories or endpoint classification accuracy. Repaired-score evaluation must name the task metric and checkpoint policy, use candidate selection data separately from final evaluation data, and keep last-checkpoint, best-loss-checkpoint, and fresh-retrain analyses distinct.

The large constructed common shift demonstrates non-invariance of the original ratio and a universal-theory limitation. Its magnitude is not evidence that ordinary training produces harmful shifts. Practical importance requires actual candidate gauge magnitudes and ranking/endpoint-metric comparisons; the existing stationary counterexample is a separate mathematical objection. No claim of practical screening failure follows from that construction alone.

Review scope: hand derivation of all displayed identities and inequalities; direct read of paper/main.tex:486-535 and theory-assumptions-review/review.md, with saved witness values checked in its results.json. No new numerical theorem simulation, training, external request, or full test suite was necessary for this algebraic review.
