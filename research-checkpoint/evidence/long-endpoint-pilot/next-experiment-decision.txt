The operational target is validation accuracy at the checkpoint restored by the corrected runner. image/src/util.py:run_experiment trains for at most 100 epochs, uses EarlyStopping(monitor="val_loss", patience=25, restore_best_weights=True), and returns validation accuracy at the restored weights. SearchRun selects the highest returned accuracy; ties retain the earlier incumbent. Checkpoint selection therefore uses CE, while candidate selection uses accuracy. Official test evaluation occurs only after search.

Best-across-100 validation CE is closer to this behavior than epoch-100 CE, but neither pilot target exactly matches it. The former can include epochs that finite-patience training would never reach and measures CE rather than returned accuracy. Both reuse validation data. The distinction changes the result: original epoch-1 rho is +0.952/+0.762 against epoch-100 CE and -0.762/-0.619 against best-across-100 CE. Retain both. Existing logs could support a labelled retrospective stopping/restore replay using the installed callback's exact semantics and accuracy at the selected epoch; that would not constitute independent prospective evidence.
